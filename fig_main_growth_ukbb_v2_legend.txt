Figure X (version 2, JS 2026-10-23): Height and head circumference by age and BMI in 16p11.2 and 22q11.2 CNV carriers, with the BMI effect on each outcome as a function of age.

(A-E) Height Z-score by age in non-carrier controls (A), 16p11.2 BP4-BP5 deletion carriers (B), 16p11.2 BP4-BP5 duplication carriers (C), 22q11.2 A-D deletion carriers (D) and 22q11.2 A-D duplication carriers (E), stratified by BMI (>= 70th centile, red; <= 30th centile, blue). Each point is one individual at the most recent visit with height and BMI; UK Biobank adults (ages 40-73) are open circles, and the control panel shows the clinical non-carriers plus a random 3,000 UK Biobank non-carriers. Curves are GAM fits with 95% confidence bands. The p-value in each carrier panel ("CNV x BMI p") tests whether the effect of BMI on the outcome differs between carriers and non-carriers by a constant amount across ages, with the BMI effect itself allowed to vary with age in a way shared by both groups (y ~ CNV + s(age, by CNV) + s(age, by BMI) + CNV x BMI, continuous BMI, 1 df; non-carrier controls as in the BMI-effect panels); the age-varying BMI term is needed because the BMI effect is positive in childhood and negative in adulthood, and carriers are mostly children while height controls are mostly UK Biobank adults. The control panels (A, G) are the reference and carry no test. Bold, P < 0.05.

(G-K) Head circumference Z-score by age in the same five groups, in the same order (controls, 16p11.2 deletion, 16p11.2 duplication, 22q11.2 deletion, 22q11.2 duplication), clinical cohorts only.

(A'-E', G'-K') Beside each trajectory panel, the effect of BMI on the outcome as a continuous function of age: half the difference in the predicted outcome between BMI = +1 SD and BMI = -1 SD at each age (SD of the outcome per SD of BMI), with 95% confidence bands. In the carrier rows the curves for carriers (blue) and non-carriers (grey) are read from the fitted age x BMI surface of the model that tests whether the carriers' age x BMI relation differs from that of non-carriers (y ~ CNV + s(age) + s(BMI) + s(age, by CNV) + ti(age, BMI) + ti(age, BMI, by CNV), REML); so each panel shows where in the age range the carriers depart from the non-carriers; the printed "CNV x age x BMI p" tests whether the carrier-non-carrier difference in the BMI effect changes with age (the tensor-product term above), bold when P < 0.05. In the control rows (A', G') the curve comes from the within-group model (y ~ s(age) + s(BMI) + ti(age, BMI)); the control row carries no p-values (the overall BMI effect and its age dependence in non-carriers are given in Supplementary Table X: height, BMI effect -0.07 SD per SD and age x BMI P < 1e-16; head circumference, +0.33 SD per SD and age x BMI P = 1.8e-5). Tick marks along the bottom of the carrier panels mark the ages of the carriers. Curves are drawn over the central 95% of each group's ages, and the carrier curve from the first to the last age with at least five carriers within five years, with any interior stretch lacking that support bridged by a straight segment between its neighbouring supported ages rather than by the model; the tensor-product surfaces use basis sizes 10 (age), 6 (BMI) and 6 x 5 (age x BMI) with the smoothing penalty inflated by gamma = 1.5, after the k = 15/10/10 x 10 surfaces proved too wiggly and bent at the ends of the age range. Height controls for these models are the clinical non-carriers plus 50,000 random UK Biobank non-carriers; head circumference is clinical only (UK Biobank has no head circumference), so head-circumference curves beyond about 30 years rest on few carriers.

(F, L) Height (F) and head circumference (L) in carriers with versus without an autism diagnosis, pooled across CNVs with growth-promoting effects ("overgrowth": 16p11.2 deletion and 22q11.2 duplication) and growth-restricting effects ("undergrowth": 16p11.2 duplication and 22q11.2 deletion), with SSC proband-sibling pairs as the non-carrier comparison; bars are means +/- SE, Delta and p the pooled difference, adj. the cohort- and age-adjusted estimate. Values in bold reach p < 0.05 after adjustment.

Numbers behind the BMI-effect panels: main_with_ukbb_bmi_effect_by_age.csv (per outcome, group and age: slope, SE and the test p-value). The earlier age-group bar version is kept in main_with_ukbb_offset_by_age.csv.

Scaling (JS 2026-10-27, Jon's decision): children (< 19 y; head circumference < 18 y) are scored on the external growth references (WHO height and BMI, UK90 head circumference). Every adult is scored relative to the non-carrier adults of their own cohort, adjusted for sex, age and age squared with the Box-Cox scaling used for the biobank main effects (one lambda per trait and sex, chosen by profile likelihood on the UK Biobank non-carrier fit and applied in every cohort; residual from the within-cohort fit divided by the cohort non-carrier residual SD; head circumference, which UK Biobank lacks, is left untransformed, lambda 1): the clinical cohorts are pooled, with the SSC parents and Simons VIP non-carrier family members as their reference, and UK Biobank is scored against its own non-carriers. Non-carrier adults therefore have mean 0 and SD 1 in every cohort by construction, and the adult part of each panel reads as carrier minus same-cohort non-carrier at each age. The BMI tails (70th and 30th centiles) are taken on this scale. Lambdas (UK Biobank non-carriers: height 0.65 male / 0.60 female, BMI -0.75 / -1.05) and reference sizes are printed in the run log.
