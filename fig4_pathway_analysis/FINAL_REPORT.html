Three-way convergence analysis — final report

Recurrent CNVs (Sacks et al.) × exome height genes (Kosmicki et al.) × height-decreasing effect bias

PGC joint submission · generated 2026-09-20 · R 4.6.1 · no pathway statistic recomputed

1. Summary

Across an analysis set of 102 pathways, GO:0008284 — Positive Regulation Of Cell Population Proliferation is the only pathway significant in all three tests of the asymmetric analysis: enrichment in asymmetric-CNV genes (B), enrichment of the pathway within the 207 Kosmicki height genes (C), and bias toward height-decreasing effects in the pathway's Kosmicki genes relative to all 207 Kosmicki genes (D).

It is Bonferroni significant in test C (P = 1.05×10−4 against 0.05/102 = 4.9×10−4; BH q = 0.0045), and ranks first by P in both the asymmetric-CNV test and the effect-direction test.

The same analysis run with mirror CNVs yields no convergent pathway: none of the 102 is significant in all three of A, C and D.

Analysis set (102)

The CNV gene universe is small — 215 mapped genes for GO:BP (124 asymmetric, 104 mirror) and 103 for KEGG (61 asymmetric, 49 mirror) — so a pathway with few genes in that universe cannot reach P < 0.05 in a CNV test however extreme its genes are. Pathways were therefore restricted to those carrying enough CNV genes for a CNV test to be capable of nominal significance. The threshold is derived, not chosen: for each pathway the best-case attainable P — all of its CNV-universe genes belonging to the target class — is computed from the hypergeometric distribution, and the pathway is admitted if that is below 0.05 for either CNV class.

Analysis set = 102 pathways (68 GO:BP + 34 KEGG): the union of the 48 pathways attainable for the asymmetric test (≥ 6 CNV-universe genes) and the 102 attainable for the mirror test (≥ 4). The asymmetric set is a strict subset of the mirror set, so the union is the mirror set. This one set is used for all four tests and fixes the multiple-testing burden throughout: Bonferroni = 0.05/102 = 4.9×10−4, with BH FDR computed within it. Tests C and D do not involve CNVs, so they are computed once and shared by the two convergence analyses; only the CNV test differs between them.

GO:0008284 has 10 of its genes in the CNV universe, 9 of them asymmetric and 3 mirror.

5. Suggested wording for the manuscript

We restricted the analysis to pathways carrying enough genes in the recurrent-CNV gene universe for a CNV test to be capable of nominal significance (≥ 6 of the 215 mapped genes for the asymmetric test, ≥ 4 for the mirror test; 102 pathways in union), and used this single set for all tests, so that the multiple-testing burden is set once (Bonferroni P < 4.9×10−4).

Of these 102 pathways, Positive Regulation Of Cell Population Proliferation (GO:0008284) was the only one significant in all three tests of the asymmetric analysis: enrichment among genes in asymmetric CNVs (OR = 7.04, P = 0.031), enrichment within the 207 exome-wide significant height genes (17 of 176 mapped genes, OR = 3.11, P = 1.05×10−4, Bonferroni significant, FDR = 0.0045), and a bias toward height-decreasing effects among those genes relative to all 207 (16 of 17 negative, OR = 8.70, P = 0.009). It ranked first by P in both the asymmetric-CNV test and the effect-direction test.

The nine contributing genes fall in four recurrent loci (15q13, 22q11.21, 2q11.2, 7q11.23); a locus-block permutation preserving the correlation among genes on the same CNV confirmed the excess (P = 0.033). The enrichment was unchanged after excluding the height genes themselves (OR = 7.03, P = 0.031), indicating that the CNV and exome signals are independent, and was absent in mirror CNVs (OR = 0.44, P = 0.94).

Repeating the analysis with mirror CNVs in place of asymmetric CNVs identified no pathway satisfying all three criteria. Neither CNV test showed an excess of enriched pathways in aggregate (2 asymmetric and 3 mirror pathways at P < 0.05), so we do not interpret either CNV screen as showing pathway enrichment across the CNV set; the evidence for GO:0008284 rests on its convergence across the three tests, on the Bonferroni-significant enrichment in the height gene set, and on the locus-block permutation.

7. Caveats

  • Neither CNV test shows an excess of enriched pathways in aggregate: 2 asymmetric and 3 mirror pathways at P < 0.05, and none survives correction in either. The CNV screens should not be presented as evidence of pathway enrichment in their own right.
  • Only the Kosmicki enrichment (test C) is Bonferroni and FDR significant. Tests A, B and D are nominal.
  • The contrast between the asymmetric and mirror analyses is a specificity check, not a formal test: one convergent pathway versus none is not by itself a significant difference, and the direct asymmetric-vs-mirror contrast for GO:0008284 is borderline (OR = 6.06, one-sided P = 0.059).
  • Tests C and D are not independent: both are computed on the Kosmicki gene set.
  • CHRNA7 appears in both an asymmetric (15q13.1–13.2) and a mirror (15q13.3) interval in the annotation table; it was not relabelled.

Data-quality note. The *_overlap_genes string columns in the master results table are unreliable — 1,286 asymmetric and 967 mirror rows carry a gene string whose length disagrees with that row's own overlap_n, and many are blank. Gene-level results here are therefore reconstructed from the source GMT files plus the two annotated gene tables, and validated against the master overlap_n columns (0 mismatches across all 5,712 pathways for the Kosmicki, asymmetric and mirror sets). make_intersection_table.R re-runs that validation and stops on any mismatch.
