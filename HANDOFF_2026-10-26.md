## 14. Prime-panel curves refitted: separate varying-coefficient models (JS 2026-10-27)
Jon spotted that C' (16p11.2 dup height) showed a positive childhood BMI effect while panel C and the raw 2-9 y slope (-0.37)
are negative. Cause: the joint carrier+control tensor model (y ~ CNV + s(age) + s(BMI) + s(age, by CNV) + ti(age, BMI) +
ti(age, BMI, by CNV)) had no carrier-specific BMI term, so every carrier curve was pulled onto the control curve (50,000 UKBB
adults). Fitting the tensor separately in carriers did not fix it (+0.09 at 2-9 y: the penalty smoothed the carriers'
childhood slope away). `40_test_bmi_curve_models.R` compared tensor and varying-coefficient (VC) models against the raw
within-band slopes (`test_bmi_curve_models.csv`): VC y ~ s(age, k = 10) + s(age, k = 8, by = BMI), gamma 1, fitted separately
in each group, agreed best (mean |curve - raw| 0.08 vs 0.11; 16p11.2 dup 2-9 y -0.22). Smoother VC variants (gamma 1.5/2,
P-spline) flatten that childhood slope to -0.05 to -0.09, so the adult wiggles of B'/C' were accepted (wide CIs).
`17_main_with_ukbb.R` now uses fit1() (VC, K_VC = 8) for all prime panels, carriers and controls; threeway_p now uses ordered-
factor difference smooths including s(BMI, by = oc) (P stored in the CSVs, not shown). Figure v5 rebuilt (job 55339993);
legends and the Doc's Fig. 6 Methods/legend updated. Supplementary Tables 21-22 unaffected (age-constant and age-band models).
