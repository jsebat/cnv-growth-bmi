Figure X. Continuous and sparse single-hit models generate distinct patterns of reciprocal CNV effects.
(A–D) Height. (A) Observed deletion (DEL) and duplication (DUP) effects from Sacks et al. show a mirror group and a lower-left displaced asymmetric group. (B) Under the continuous model, increasing small-effect directional load produces a gradual spread away from the mirror relationship. (C) Under the sparse single-hit model, increasing critical-gene frequency primarily changes the fraction of loci occupying a distinct shifted state. (D) Best-fit directional-residual distributions comparing the observed loci with the mirror-alone, continuous, and sparse single-hit models.
(E–H) BMI. (E) Observed reciprocal CNV effects form a single upper-right shifted reciprocal cloud. (F) The continuous model generates a gradual upper-right displacement of the reciprocal cloud. (G) The sparse single-hit model generates a more mixture-like pattern. (H) Best-fit directional-residual distributions. Revised BMI simulation clouds were generated from a moment-matched aggregate mirror approximation to avoid zero-inflated artifacts from weakly identifiable gene-level p and m estimates; model-comparison results are unchanged.
