Figure X. Genome-wide CNV burden predictions under mirror, continuous, and sparse single-hit models.
(A,E) Literature burden targets. For height, Macé et al. reported a negative deletion-burden coefficient and Auwerx et al. supported an adverse overall burden direction, implying DUP burden should also move height downward. For BMI, Macé et al. reported a positive deletion-burden coefficient and Auwerx et al. supported an adverse adiposity burden direction, implying DUP burden should also move BMI upward.
(B–D, F–H) Simulated burden coefficients under each fitted model. Points show simulated burden coefficients generated by varying the effective density of dosage-responsive genes per megabase around the density required to match the Macé deletion-burden coefficient. Orange X marks the matched Macé calibration point. Mirror alone predicts the wrong duplication-burden direction for both traits. For height, both the continuous and sparse single-hit models reverse the DUP prediction below zero. For BMI, both corrected upper-right models shift the DUP prediction to approximately zero or positive values.
