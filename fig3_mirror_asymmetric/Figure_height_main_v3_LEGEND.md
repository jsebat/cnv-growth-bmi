**Figure 3. Asymmetry is a rare single hit on an oligogenic mirror background.** Analyses include 33 reciprocal CNV loci with published height effects (21 mirror, blue; 12 asymmetric, red). (**A**) Perpendicular offsets from the mirror line (DUP = −0.351 × DEL) follow a bimodal distribution. Curves show measurement-error-aware one- and two-component mixture fits; rug marks indicate published classes. (**B**) Parametric bootstrap test of bimodality (5,000 replicates; observed likelihood ratio = 9.9, *p* = 0.016). (**C–F**) Asymmetric CNVs are longer (*p* = 0.006), contain more genes (*p* = 0.019), have comparable deletion effects (*p* = 0.651) but more negative duplication effects (*p* = 10⁻⁶). Bars indicate medians. (**G, H**) After stratifying mirror CNVs from asymmetric CNVs, gene count correlates with effect size negatively for deletion and positively for duplication (Spearman ρ = −0.61 and +0.51, respectively), but not for asymmetric loci. Lines show Theil–Sen fits, including pooled estimates (black dotted). (**I**) The dosage effect of each CNV on height, half the difference between its duplication and deletion effects, against the summed transcriptome-wide association (TWAS) z scores of its genes (common-variant predicted expression; Mancuso et al. 2017). The dosage effect cancels a shift shared by both alleles and halves one acting on duplications alone, so it largely removes the single-hit component. Mirror and asymmetric CNVs follow one relationship (separate lines *p* = 0.25; all CNVs ρ = 0.69, *p* = 0.0008 by circular genomic permutation). Open square, the Williams–Beuren region (7q11.23), excluded from the line (Theil–Sen fit) and from all correlations. (**J**) Neither class carries unusual gene content for its size: each CNV's summed TWAS z relative to random genomic windows with the same number of genes (mirror *p* = 0.79, asymmetric *p* = 0.21, difference *p* = 0.45). I and J use non-overlapping CNVs only (subregions of larger analysed intervals omitted). (**K, L**) Effects simulated under each directional model, pooled over 150 datasets generated with the observed gene counts and standard errors, with observed loci overlaid in black. The continuous model displaces every locus in proportion to gene count (ΔAIC 2.6), filling the gap between classes; the single-hit model displaces only loci carrying a hit (36% at the median gene count; ΔAIC 0.0), generating two separated clouds. (**M–O**) Comparison of mirror-only, continuous and single-hit models. (**M**) Both directional models reproduce the association of duplication burden with short stature observed in previous studies (Macé et al. 2017), unlike mirror-only. (**N**) Only the single-hit model predicts two distinct clusters of mirror and asymmetric CNVs. (**O**) Simulated likelihood ratios show that single-hit, but not continuous or mirror-only, typically reproduces the observed bimodality (median *p* = 0.014, 0.308 and 0.423, respectively). Effects are in SD units unless otherwise indicated. Statistics for all panels are given in Supplementary Tables 25 and 29, and the analyses repeated without the 9 loci lacking a nominal deletion or duplication effect in Supplementary Table 26.

Numbers in I–J are for --twas-set ad (22q11.2 A–D). For --twas-set fig3 (22q11.2 A–B and B–D): ρ = 0.62, *p* = 0.002; separate lines *p* = 0.18; J mirror *p* = 0.90, asymmetric *p* = 0.17, difference *p* = 0.35.
