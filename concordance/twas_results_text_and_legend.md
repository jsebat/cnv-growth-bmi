# TWAS concordance: plain-language Results paragraph and panel legend (draft v4, 2026-11-02: 23 non-overlapping CNVs, spanning intervals kept)

## Results (about 190 words)
Do the genes inside a CNV predict which way it shifts height? For each gene, transcriptome-wide association studies (TWAS) estimate whether people whose genotypes predict higher expression of that gene tend to be taller or shorter. Adding these estimates across the genes in a CNV gives a prediction for the CNV as a whole: if its genes are, on balance, height-raising, extra copies should make people taller and missing copies shorter. This prediction matched the observed dosage effects of CNVs on height (Spearman ρ = 0.69; P = 0.0008 against random placement of the CNVs in the genome; Fig. Xa). Mirror and asymmetric CNVs followed the same relationship, with one exception, the Williams–Beuren region (7q11.23), whose deletion shortens stature far more than its genes predict. The agreement did not depend on the number of genes in a CNV, and higher expression was not generally associated with greater height. What gene content did not predict was how far a CNV departs from a mirror response, the feature that defines asymmetric CNVs, consistent with that departure arising from a single gene of large effect (Supplementary Note 4). There was no agreement for BMI.

## Dosage effect and the single hit (Jon 2026-11-02; numbers from twas_hit_cancellation.py)
We summarized each CNV by its dosage effect, half the difference between its duplication and deletion effects. A gene that lowers height whenever its copy number changes shifts deletion and duplication effects in the same direction and cancels in this difference, and a single hit acting on duplications alone is halved. The dosage effect therefore isolates the contribution of ordinary dosage-sensitive genes. Consistent with this, once gene content was taken into account, asymmetric CNVs sat only slightly below mirror CNVs on this scale (−0.1 s.d., P = 0.3), and both classes followed the same relationship with their genes' predicted effects.

Note: exact cancellation holds only for a shift equal on both alleles; the Fig. 3 single hit (mean 0.28 s.d., mainly on the duplication axis) is halved (about −0.14 s.d.), matching the observed non-significant offset of −0.10 to −0.14 s.d.
